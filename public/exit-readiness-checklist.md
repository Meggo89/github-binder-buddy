# Exit Readiness Checklist

**Twelve questions every UK owner should answer before starting an M&A process.**

Mastella Advisory · Confidential exit and capital advisory for founder-led businesses worth £5M–£50M.

---

## 1. Financial readiness

**Can you produce three years of monthly management accounts that reconcile cleanly to your statutory accounts, with a defensible adjusted-EBITDA bridge?**

Buyers will rebuild your EBITDA from the numbers you give them. Every adjustment you make (owner salary normalisation, personal expenses, one-off items) will be tested. Founders who arrive with a bridge already documented, backed by supporting evidence, consistently retain more of the value they claim. Founders who leave adjustments undocumented consistently lose them.

---

## 2. Operational readiness

**Could your business run without you for six months? Not "in theory" — actually?**

Buyers pay for cash flow that survives the owner's departure. If your senior team cannot make buying decisions, negotiate with suppliers, or resolve major customer issues without you, that is a discount to the multiple — usually a large one. Building a genuine number-two 12–24 months pre-process is one of the highest-return pieces of preparation any owner can do.

---

## 3. Owner dependency

**Which of your top 20 customer relationships are held personally by you rather than by the business?**

This is the specific version of question 2. Buyer diligence will map each anchor customer to a named relationship holder. Where that name is yours and yours only, the concentration flag becomes a founder-dependency flag as well — and both discount price. Broadening customer relationships across the senior team, and documenting it, is often the single most valuable pre-process intervention.

---

## 4. Contract quality

**How many of your top 20 customer contracts contain change-of-control clauses that could be triggered by a sale?**

Change-of-control clauses give customers the right to walk when ownership changes. Buyers will find them in diligence whether or not you flag them. Where they exist, the answers to "who has one, what does it require, and what is our plan" need to be ready before the data room opens — not discovered in it.

---

## 5. Working capital

**What is the trailing 12-month average of your working capital, and is that number stable or trending?**

Working capital normalisation is the single most disputed line item in completion mechanics for lower mid-market UK M&A. Owners who arrive at exchange with a documented normalised working capital position — ideally one that has been reviewed by their own accountants — save weeks of negotiation and often meaningful cash at completion.

---

## 6. IP protection

**Is every material piece of IP in the business — code, brand, methodology, data assets — owned by the company, with a clean chain of assignment from every contractor and former employee who touched it?**

Historic IP built by contractors under unclear ownership terms is one of the most common late-stage diligence flags. It rarely kills a deal, but it consistently costs weeks of remediation and sometimes chunks of consideration. Clean IP ownership documentation is a small pre-process job with a large downside if skipped.

---

## 7. Key-person risk

**If your top three senior team members left in the six months after completion, what would happen to your EBITDA?**

Buyers ask this question in explicit form. Their answer, based on their diligence, drives the retention arrangements they will insist on. Yours, thought through before the process starts, tells you where lock-ins, retention bonuses or equity arrangements need to be in place before those team members are asked to accept them under transaction pressure.

---

## 8. Buyer profile

**Have you formed a view — from evidence, not assumption — of who the three most likely buyers of your business are, and why?**

Owners who have thought about the buyer pool before running a process consistently make better decisions during it. Strategic vs PE, UK vs overseas, consolidator vs platform-play buyer — each implies different valuation methodology, different diligence emphasis, and different post-sale trajectory for you personally.

---

## 9. Timing

**What are the three biggest reasons the next 24 months are the right window (or the wrong window) for a sale?**

Timing decisions are usually driven by a mix of personal, financial, sector and macro factors. Writing them down honestly — including the reasons NOT to sell now — makes the eventual go/no-go decision cleaner. It also surfaces the areas where waiting 6–12 months materially improves outcome vs where waiting materially harms it.

---

## 10. Deal structure

**What proportion of consideration are you willing to accept as deferred, and under what conditions?**

The lower mid-market rarely offers 100% cash at completion. Understanding where you sit on deferred consideration (vendor loan, earn-out, deferred preferred equity, retained equity in a rollover) — including the tax and risk implications of each — before you enter negotiation dramatically reduces the chance of being pushed into a structure that does not suit you.

---

## 11. Tax

**Have you spoken to a transaction-tax specialist about your position at least 12 months before the target sale window?**

Pre-sale tax structuring is one of the highest-ROI activities in any exit — but many of the most useful interventions require a 12-month runway before completion, and some (particularly around share reorganisations and reliefs) require longer. Waiting until a specific deal is on the table typically closes off the highest-value options.

---

## 12. Life after exit

**What does the first 12 months after completion look like — for you personally, financially and professionally?**

Founders who cannot answer this question in detail routinely find themselves accepting terms during negotiation (post-sale role, earn-out linkage to their continued involvement, lock-ins on non-compete) that do not match what they actually want. Getting clear on the post-exit plan before running the process is the single most reliable way to make good decisions inside it.
